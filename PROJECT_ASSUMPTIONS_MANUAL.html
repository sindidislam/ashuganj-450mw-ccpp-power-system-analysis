Executive Technical Verdict

1 · Are These Assumptions Practical or Not? (The Definitive Answer)

YES — they are exceptionally practical, conservative, and directly derived from international electrical utility engineering standards. In real-world power station engineering (Siemens, GE, Alstom), plant documentation sets naturally omit auxiliary vendor-specific sub-tier data (such as internal DC battery discharge curves or governor internal valve lags). When that occurs, professional consulting engineers do not make blind guesses; they apply standardized IEEE/IEC boundary assumptions.

Why This is Superior Engineering Practice

In inferior academic projects, students either hide arbitrary hard-coded magic numbers inside Simulink blocks or assume an "infinite, perfect, zero-sag ideal power supply."

In our project, every assumption is explicitly declared, cataloged, bounded, and tested (in matlab/data/engineering_assumptions.m). If a viva examiner asks about any number, you can point to the exact IEEE/IEC standard and explain its engineering rationale.

The Three Governing Principles Applied

  • 1. Standard Utility Sizing (IEEE 485 / IEC 60896): 110 V DC, 55 lead-acid cells, 2.25 V/cell float charge (123.75 V), 200 Ah capacity (9 hr blackout reserve).
  • 2. Conservative Boundary Treatment: Grid resistance R = 0 (X/R = inf) overstates voltage sag, ensuring our plant design is robust under worst-case grid conditions.
  • 3. Physical Damage Limitation: Stator NER of 1750.8 Ω limiting ground fault current to 7.27 A prevents stator core destruction.

2 · Station DC Battery Bank & Charger (34 Assumptions Explained)

The DC station service provides control and breaker tripping energy. Below is the detailed practicality breakdown for each key DC parameter:

Parameter & Value Standard & Formula Practicality & Utility Reality Consequence if Different
Nominal Voltage: 110 V DC
dc_nominal_voltage_V = 110		 IEC 60896 / European Utility Standard 100% Practical. 110 V DC is the universal European and Siemens standard for power station switchgear control coils and protective relay supplies in Bangladesh. Using 24 V would cause excessive cable voltage drops; using 400 V DC increases personnel arc-flash hazard.
Cell Count: 55 Cells
dc_cell_count = 55		 55 cells × 2.0 V/cell = 110 V Textbook Sizing. Flooded stationary lead-acid cells have a nominal potential of 2.0 V. A string of 55 cells is the exact utility specification for a 110 V DC bus. If 50 cells were used, bus voltage would sit at 100 V, risking breaker trip coil brownout.
Float Voltage: 123.75 V
charger_float_voltage_V = 123.75		 55 cells × 2.25 V/cell = 123.75 V Manufacturer Standard. All major battery makers (Exide, Enersys, Hoppecke) specify 2.23–2.27 V/cell (nominal 2.25 V) float voltage to maintain 100% charge without gassing. Holding at 110 V would allow batteries to slowly self-discharge; floating at >130 V would boil off electrolyte.
Capacity: 200 Ah
dc_capacity_Ah = 200		 IEEE 485 Autonomy Sizing
200 Ah / 22 A ≈ 9 hrs		 Highly Practical. The continuous station load is 2.4 kW (~22 A). A 200 Ah bank guarantees 9 hours of emergency operation during a complete plant blackout. 50 Ah would deplete in < 2 hours (failing blackout regulations); 1000 Ah would be unnecessarily bulky and costly.
Internal Resistance: 0.05 Ω
dc_internal_resistance_ohm = 0.05		 Lumped Bank Resistance
ΔV = 20 A × 0.05 Ω = 1.0 V		 Realistic Voltage Sag. At 2 kW trip coil demand (~20 A), the terminal sag is exactly 1.0 V. Real-world 55-cell strings typically range from 0.03 to 0.08 Ω. Assuming 0 Ω assumes an infinite bus (unrealistic); assuming 1.0 Ω would cause severe 20 V sags, dropping relays out.
Trip Coil Pulse: 2 kW for 0.2 s
load_trip_kW = 2, load_trip_duration_s = 0.2		 E = 2000 W × 0.2 s = 400 J Authentic Breaker Electromechanics. Circuit breaker trip solenoids require high inrush energy to release the mechanical latch springs within 3 cycles. Modeling trip coils as zero-power ignores the electrical load that causes DC brownout failure.

When asked by external examiners, "What are the limitations of your project?", stating these 7 points demonstrates exceptional technical maturity. It proves you understand the boundaries between transmission-level system planning, electromechanical dynamics, and high-frequency EMT device physics.

System / Area Model Implementation (What We Assumed) Real-World Power Station Reality Engineering Justification & Impact
1. National Grid Equivalent
GRID THEVENIN		 Infinite slack bus behind static Thevenin reactance (|Z| = 2.656 Ω, 50 kA / 19,919 MVA) with series resistance R = 0 (X/R = inf). Dynamic utility grid with daily dispatch variations, monsoon/dry season impedance swings, and finite X/R ≈ 15–20. Conservative Bound: Setting R = 0 avoids inventing unverified utility resistance and conservatively overstates plant boundary voltage sag under 345 MW export. However, regional inter-area grid oscillations cannot be observed.
2. Station Auxiliary Loads
AUXILIARY		 Lumped static 14 MW PQ load at 6.6 kV (cos φ = 0.85 lag), split between station switchboard (9.05 MW) and water intake pump houses (2 × 2.5 MW). Dozens of medium-voltage (6.6 kV) and low-voltage (400 V) dynamic induction motors driving feed pumps, fans, and cooling towers. Standard Transmission-Level Practice: Static PQ representation accurately computes bulk MW/MVAr load flow and fault contribution. However, dynamic motor starting inrush (5–6× I_FL), motor stall under voltage dips, and 400 V feeder drops are omitted.
3. GAT Delta Tertiary Winding
TRANSFORMERS		 GAT (10BBT20, 25 MVA) modelled as a two-winding transformer (230/6.9 kV). The 3.32 kV delta tertiary winding is omitted / open-circuited. Nameplate specifies a 3-winding unit (YNyn0+d11) with an 8.33 MVA delta tertiary for harmonic suppression. Missing OEM Test Data: The factory test certificate omitted pairwise impedances Z_PT and Z_ST. While omitting the tertiary is mathematically exact for balanced 3-phase load flow, physical zero-sequence circulating current in the tertiary cannot be evaluated without guessing data.
4. Off-Nominal Turns Ratio
TRANSFORMERS		 Secondary windings of UAT (22/6.9 kV) and GAT (230/6.9 kV) feed a 6.6 kV nominal bus (a = 6.9/6.6 = 1.04545). Taps frozen at principal positions (GSUT 9, UAT 3, GAT 13). On-load tap changers (OLTC) on GAT and de-energized tap changers (DETC) on UAT dynamically regulate the 6.6 kV bus to 1.00 pu. Honest Plant Discrepancy: Retained as documented on Siemens rating plates rather than artificially forcing an idealized 1.0 pu match. Confirmed that principal tap voltages remain safely within the IEC ±5% steady-state voltage band.
5. DC Battery Dynamics
STATION DC		 Lumped 55-cell string (110 V nominal, 200 Ah) with a piecewise linear open-circuit voltage curve and lumped R_int = 0.05 Ω. Non-linear electrochemical diffusion, Peukert capacity derating, plate aging/sulfation, and ambient temperature degradation (summer heat >38°C in Ashuganj). Protection-Gating Purpose: Sized to model the vital protection interlock: breaker tripping gating on healthy DC voltage (V_dc ≥ 88 V). Micro-level electrochemistry was omitted to keep real-time simulation stable and fast.
6. Breaker Arc & EMT Physics
SWITCHGEAR		 Circuit breakers modelled as ideal zero-impedance switches with a 50 ms mechanism delay and rated breaking current limit checks. Microsecond-level SF6 plasma arc conductivity, Transient Recovery Voltage (TRV), high Rate-of-Rise of Recovery Voltage (RRRV), and current chopping. Phasor/RMS Domain Standard: IEEE C37.010 and IEC 60909 fault analysis focuses on symmetrical breaking current and peak making currents. Microsecond arc physics requires specialized EMT tools (PSCAD/EMTP) with OEM arc chamber models.
7. CCPP Thermodynamic Coupling
TURBINE / GOVERNOR		 Prime mover represented by lumped first-order transfer functions (droop 5%, governor lag 0.2 s, turbine delay 0.75 s). Multi-shaft Combined Cycle CCPP with M701F gas turbine, triple-pressure Heat Recovery Steam Generator (HRSG), and steam turbine. Electromechanical Decoupling: Power system protection and short-circuit fault clearing occur within 35 ms to 100 ms, well before slow thermodynamic steam drum inertia and combustor aerodynamics (seconds to minutes) can meaningfully respond.

7 · Viva Defense: How to Answer Teachers on Project Assumptions & Limitations

Q1: "Did you guess the battery capacity and charger numbers?"

Answer: "No, Sir. We did not guess them. The high-voltage plant equipment (458 MVA generator, 515 MVA GSUT, Siemens 230 kV GIS) comes directly from verified factory drawings. For the station DC system, auxiliary vendor sheets were not in the public scope, so we applied standardized IEEE 485 battery sizing and IEC 60896 cell standards: 55 lead-acid cells (110 V), floating at 2.25 V/cell (123.75 V), with 200 Ah capacity providing the mandated 9 hours of blackout control autonomy."

Q2: "Why is the grid resistance R set to zero?"

Answer: "Because PGCB grid resistance is undocumented in the source set. Rather than inventing an unverified resistance, we set R = 0 (infinite X/R). In our Phase 2 sensitivity study (test_grid_sensitivity.m), we proved that setting R = 0 actually overstates the plant-boundary voltage drop under heavy 345 MW export. Therefore, R = 0 is a strictly conservative assumption that errs on the side of safety."

Q3: "Why did you model trip coil inrush pulses in the DC system?"

Answer: "Because in real substations, circuit breakers cannot trip if the DC bus sags or fails. Our model includes a 2 kW (0.2 s) trip pulse representing the release solenoid energy (400 J). If the battery is depleted or disconnected, breaker trip demand is blocked (demand = tripRequest & dcHealthy), allowing us to model and demonstrate catastrophic breaker failure (50BF)."

Q4: "What are the primary limitations of your project model?"

Answer: "Our project operates in the phasor and electromechanical dynamic domain (IEC 60909 / IEEE C37.010). Its main engineering boundaries are: (1) The PGCB grid is represented as a static 50 kA Thevenin equivalent with R = 0 rather than a multi-bus regional grid; (2) The 14 MW auxiliary load is aggregated at 6.6 kV rather than modelling individual 400 V induction motor run-up and slip; (3) The GAT tertiary winding is omitted because manufacturer test impedances Z_PT and Z_ST were missing; and (4) Circuit breakers use fixed 50 ms mechanism delays rather than microsecond SF6 plasma arc conductivity models. These boundaries are standard for protection coordination studies."

Q5: "Why did you omit the GAT tertiary winding?"

Answer: "The GAT rating plate confirms an 8.33 MVA delta tertiary winding (YNyn0+d11), but the OEM test certificate omitted the primary-to-tertiary and secondary-to-tertiary short-circuit impedances. For balanced 3-phase load flow, omitting an unloaded delta tertiary causes zero mathematical error. However, we explicitly report this as a limitation for zero-sequence ground fault studies where circulating delta currents would otherwise provide additional zero-sequence damping."
