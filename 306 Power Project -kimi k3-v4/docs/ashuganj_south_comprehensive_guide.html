Single-Line Diagram (SLD) & Operating Dynamics

Physical power flow topology from generation terminals to the national transmission grid.

1. Normal Generating Mode

Case LF1 / LF3

Power Export: Generator G1 supplies ~389.3 MW at 22 kV. Power flows across GSUT (230/22 kV) into the 230 kV GIS and exports ~374.5 MW to the national grid.

House Load: The UAT (22/6.9 kV) taps ~14.0 MW from the 22 kV generator bus to power auxiliary pumps, fans, and compressors.

GAT State: Bay 10BAY20 breaker is OPEN on standby.

Most efficient steady state: No circulating loop power.

2. Blackout / Plant Startup Mode

Offline / Starting

Startup Supply: With G1 offline (0 V), the plant draws electricity from the 230 kV national grid through GAT bay 10BAY20 and the GAT transformer (230/6.9 kV).

Auxiliary Drives: GAT powers the Static Frequency Converter (SFC), cranking motors, boiler feed pumps, and fuel compressors to spin the turbine up to ignition speed.

UAT remains de-energized until G1 is synchronized to grid.

3. Looped / Paralleled Mode

Case LF2 / LF4

The Closed Loop: If GAT bay 10BAY20 is closed while UAT is energized, a closed parallel loop is formed across 22 kV and 230 kV.

Circulating Detour: Due to transformer ratio and impedance differences, ~5.9 MW detours down through UAT and circulates upwards through GAT, overloading UAT to 109.5% ONAN.

Interlocks normally prevent continuous steady-state paralleling.

Critical Finding 1

UAT Overload in Looped Mode is Circulating Loop Power

In Case LF2, total auxiliary demand remains exactly 14.0 MW, yet the UAT carries 19.91 MW / 20.81 MVA (109.50% of its ONAN rating). Due to transformer ratio and impedance mismatch across the loop, 5.9 MW of export power detours down through the UAT and flows BACK UP through the GAT into the 230 kV busbar.

What to Tell Teachers

"The UAT overload is circulating power caused by paralleling two transformers with different vector groups and ratios. This physical finding proves why modern power plants strictly interlock the UAT and GAT against continuous parallel operation."

Design Finding 2

GSUT Requires Active Forced Cooling (ODAF) at Rated Dispatch

At 389.30 MW gross generation (LF1), the GSUT carries 375.82 MVA, reaching 105.87% of its natural ONAN stage (355 MVA). Against its maximum forced oil/air rating (515 MVA ODAF), it is loaded to only 72.97% (27% thermal margin).

What to Tell Teachers

"This is an intentional design feature. Full rated power requires the transformer's forced oil circulation and cooling fans (ODAF). The 355 MVA ONAN rating is not intended for continuous full-plant dispatch."

Network Finding 3

Plant Exports Real Power (MW) but Absorbs Reactive Power (MVAr)

$Q_{\text{exp}} = -29.99\text{ MVAr}$ (negative export = grid import). The GSUT series inductive reactance alone absorbs 43.86 MVAr. Because the plant has no capacitor banks, the generator's +31.68 MVAr output covers internal needs while remaining reactive losses are absorbed from the 230 kV grid.

What to Tell Teachers

"Say 'the plant absorbs MVAr', not 'exports negative MVAr'. The absorption is predominantly due to transformer inductive reactance losses ($I^2X$), which is typical for thermal plants without shunt compensation."

Capacity Finding 4

230 kV GIS Switchyard Has Enormous Thermal Headroom

The maximum continuous current across the 230 kV busbars is 943 A, which represents only 30.0% of the 3,150 A Siemens 8DN9 continuous rating.

What to Tell Teachers

"The switchgear is nowhere near thermal capacity. It was dimensioned to withstand 50 kA / 1 s short-circuit fault currents rather than continuous steady-state current limits."

Remaining Project Scope (Phase 2 Roadmap)

Now that the steady-state base load flow is fully validated, the project transitions into fault analysis and protection coordination.

1

Symmetrical Fault Analysis

Apply 3-phase (LLL) short-circuits at 22 kV generator bus, 230 kV GIS, and 6.6 kV MV bus to calculate maximum symmetrical sub-transient fault currents ($I''_{k}$).

2

Unsymmetrical Fault Analysis

Simulate Line-to-Ground (LG), Line-to-Line (LL), and Double Line-to-Ground (LLG) faults using zero-sequence transformer impedances ($Z_0$) and neutral grounding resistors (NER).

3

Circuit Breaker Duty Sizing

Compare calculated symmetrical and asymmetrical breaking currents against switchgear ratings (50 kA at 230 kV, 12 kA at 22 kV) to verify interrupt capability.

4

Overcurrent Relay Coordination

Calculate precise Plug Settings (PS) and Time Multiplier Settings (TMS) for IEC standard inverse (SI) relays, ensuring time grading margin $\Delta t \ge 0.3\text{ s}$.

5

Differential Protection (87G/87T)

Implement biased differential protection schemes for generator (87G), GSUT (87T), and UAT, accounting for vector group phase compensation and CT saturation.

6

Selectivity Verification

Re-simulate fault events in Simulink Fault Analyzer to verify that relays isolate only the faulted zone without a total plant blackout.
