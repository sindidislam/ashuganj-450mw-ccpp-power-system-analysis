Protection Architecture

1 · Protection Philosophy & The 4 Defensive Pillars

The protective relay system is structured into four distinct, overlapping defense zones to guarantee 100% selectivity, zero uncoordinated blackouts, and sub-100 ms fault clearing:

Pillar 1 · Unit Differential
87G & 87T
45 ms Instantaneous Clearing
Pillar 2 · Bus & Line Diff
87B & 87L
35–40 ms Fiber/Spill Clearing
Pillar 3 · Graded Backup
51 Overcurrent
Strict 300 ms CTI Coordination
Pillar 4 · Ground Defense
51N + NER
Crushes Ground Fault to 7.27 A
  • Zone 1 — Generator Zone (87G): Bounded by neutral CTs and GCB CTs. Detects internal stator winding faults without intentional delay.
  • Zone 2 — Transformer Zone (87T): Bounded by GCB CTs and 230 kV GIS CTs. Protects the 515 MVA GSUT against internal turn-to-turn and bushing flashovers with vector group (YNd1, −30°) compensation.
  • Zone 3 — Switchyard Busbar Zone (87B): Decentralized busbar differential protecting 230 kV GIS Bus 1 and Bus 2, clearing severe 50.53 kA bus faults in 35 ms.
  • Zone 4 — Transmission Line Zone (87L & 21): Optical fiber line differential (87L) clearing 50% line faults in 40 ms, backed up by 3-zone distance protection (21).

Mathematical Rigor

4 · Mathematical Derivation of Key Protection Settings

1. Generator Time-Overcurrent (GEN-51)

$$ ext{Machine Maximum Continuous MVA} = 518 ext{ MVA}, \quad V_{ ext{min}} = 22 ext{ kV} imes 0.95 = 20.9 ext{ kV}$$ $$I_{ ext{max}} = rac{518 imes 10^6}{\sqrt{3} imes 20,900} = 14,309.28 ext{ A}$$ $$I_{ ext{pickup}} = 1.20 imes 14,309.28 = \mathbf{17,170.8 ext{ A primary}} \quad \longrightarrow \quad I_{ ext{sec}} = rac{17,170.8}{15,000} = \mathbf{1.1447 ext{ A}}$$ Enforcing $ ext{TMS} = 0.10$ on the IEC Normal Inverse curve clears a 126.21 kA fault in 0.594 s, maintaining a 1.76 s safety margin above downstream relays.

2. Sensitive Ground Protection & The 7.27 A NER Story (GEN-51N)

Single-phase grounding transformer ratio: $n = rac{22,000 / \sqrt{3}}{500} = 25.4034$.
Referred secondary resistor: $R_{ ext{ref}} = n^2 imes 2.62\ \Omega = (25.4034)^2 imes 2.62 = 1,690.8\ \Omega$.
Total loop zero-sequence resistance: $R_{ ext{loop}} = 60\ \Omega ext{ (primary)} + 1,690.8\ \Omega = \mathbf{1,750.8\ \Omega}$.
Prospective 1-phase ground fault current: $$I_{ ext{LG}} = rac{22,000 / \sqrt{3}}{1,750.8\ \Omega} = \mathbf{7.27 ext{ A}}$$ Because 7.27 A on a 15,000/1 A CT gives only 0.48 mA (blind), we installed a 20/1 Neutral CT: $$I_{ ext{sec}} = rac{7.27 ext{ A}}{20} = \mathbf{0.3635 ext{ A}} \quad ( ext{Operating Margin} = 1.82 imes ext{ against } 0.20 ext{ A pickup})$$ Tripping time at $ ext{TMS} = 0.15$ is 1.746 s, safely extinguishing the arc before stator lamination iron burns.

3. GSUT-HV-51 & Coordination Time Interval (CTI = 300 ms)

Nominal 458 MVA full-load current on 230 kV side: $$I_{ ext{load}} = rac{458 imes 10^6}{\sqrt{3} imes 230,000} = 1,149.68 ext{ A} \quad \longrightarrow \quad I_{ ext{pickup}} = 1.20 imes 1,149.68 = \mathbf{1,380.0 ext{ A}}$$ Secondary current on 1,600/1 A CT: $I_{ ext{sec}} = rac{1,380}{1,600} = \mathbf{0.8625 ext{ A}}$.
Setting $ ext{TMS} = 0.55$ ensures that for any downstream fault on the 230 kV switchyard or lines, GSUT-HV-51 waits at least 300 ms longer than the downstream bay breaker, preventing uncoordinated plant shutdowns.
Closed-Loop Control

5 · Circuit Breaker Mechanism Delays & Station DC Trip Interlock

  • Physical 50 ms Breaker Opening Mechanism: Real SF6 circuit breakers do not open in zero time. The Level-2 S-function block enforces a certified 50 ms delay (C.breakerDelay_s = 0.050 s) representing the mechanical latch release, spring discharge, contact parting, and SF6 gas puffer arc extinction.
  • Station DC Health Supervisory Gating: Protection relays command trips, but the station battery supplies the physical solenoid energy:
    Demand = TripRequest & dcHealthy
    If DC bus voltage sags below 88 V (0.80 pu), the trip coil cannot release the mechanical latch.
  • 2 kW Release Solenoid Energy Pulse: The breaker control model injects a real-world 2 kW (0.2 s, 400 J) pulse into the 110 V DC bus during every trip request, verifying that station batteries do not experience a brownout under multiple simultaneous breaker trips.
  • Current Extinction Criterion: Current interruption is verified when phase current crosses zero and stays below max(1 A, 0.1% pre-fault) for a full 20 ms post-arc duration.

Exam Preparation

8 · Comprehensive Viva Q&A: Protection Relays & Settings

Q1: "Why do we need a dedicated 20/1 Neutral CT when we already have 15,000/1 A phase CTs?"

Answer: "Because of the Phase CT Blindness problem. The 1,750.8 Ω Neutral Grounding Resistor (NER) limits stator ground faults to 7.27 A. When 7.27 A passes through a 15,000/1 A CT, the secondary current is only 0.485 mA, which is far below any relay's measurement sensitivity. By adding a dedicated 20/1 Neutral CT, 7.27 A produces 0.3635 A secondary, which is 1.82 times the 0.20 A pickup setting of Relay 51N, ensuring positive, fast trip operation."

Q2: "Why is Busbar Differential Protection (87B) so critical on the 230 kV GIS bus?"

Answer: "Because prospective 3-phase fault current on the 230 kV bus is 50.53 kA. Relay 87B detects the internal spill current and trips in 35 ms (total clearing ~90 ms). If 87B did not exist, the backup overcurrent relay (Q0-51) would take 7.28 seconds to trip due to coordination grading. Allowing a 50 kA electric arc to burn inside an SF6 enclosure for 7 seconds would cause an explosive rupture of the GIS hall."

Q3: "How does the GSUT Delta winding protect the generator during 230 kV ground faults?"

Answer: "The GSUT vector group is $YNd1$. The 230 kV side is star-grounded ($YN$), while the 22 kV generator side is delta ($d$). Zero-sequence ground currents caused by 230 kV ground faults circulate harmlessly inside the closed delta winding and cannot cross the magnetic barrier into the 22 kV bus. Thus, 230 kV ground faults cannot inject ground current into the generator stator."

Q4: "Why did you add a 50 ms breaker delay and DC voltage interlock in Simulink?"

Answer: "In simple academic projects, breakers open in zero seconds, which is physically impossible. Real Siemens SF6 breakers have mechanical spring release and arc-extinction times of 50 ms (~2.5 cycles). Furthermore, circuit breakers cannot trip if station DC battery voltage collapses. Our model enforces demand = tripRequest & dcHealthy ($V_{dc} \ge 88 ext{ V}$), verifying that the 110 V battery bank can deliver the 2 kW solenoid trip pulse."
