Phase 2 & 3 Analysis

2 · Load Flow & Power Export Findings

  • Net Active Power Export: At the primary study dispatch (360.0 MW Gross), the plant supplies 14.0 MW to internal auxiliaries and exports 345.19 MW into the PGCB national grid at the 230 kV boundary.
  • Generator Reactive Power: Operates comfortably at 27.83 MVAr lagging (Power Factor = 0.997), well inside the manufacturer's P-Q capability curve (retaining over 213 MVAr of reserve margin).
  • 230 kV Switchyard Voltage Profile: Bus 1 and Bus 2 operate at 229.71 kV (0.9988 pu), fully complying with the Bangladesh Grid Code allowable steady-state voltage window (±5% or 0.95 to 1.05 pu).
  • Plant Internal Losses: Total plant transmission and step-up copper/core losses equal 0.805 MW (0.22%), confirming high thermal and electrical efficiency.
  • Substation Coupler Status: The GIS bus coupler breaker (10BAY13) is normally CLOSED, tying Bus 1 and Bus 2 in parallel, balancing current equally between the dual-circuit transmission lines.

Phase 5 Protection Coordination

5 · Protective Relay Speeds & Coordination Findings

  • High-Speed Differential Protection (ANSI 87):
    • 87B (Busbar Differential): Trips in 35 ms (0.035 s) for switchyard busbar faults.
    • 87L (Line Differential): Trips in 40 ms (0.040 s) over optical fiber communication.
    • 87G (Generator Differential): Trips in 45 ms (0.045 s) for internal stator faults.
    • 87T (Transformer Differential): Trips in 45 ms (0.045 s) for GSUT internal faults.
  • Total Fault Clearing Time (< 100 ms):
    Relay detection (35–45 ms) + SF6 breaker mechanism opening (50 ms) = 85–95 ms total clearance. This is well within the critical rotor stability limit (100 ms), completely preventing loss of synchronism.
  • Selective Overcurrent Coordination (ANSI 51):
    Enforces a strict 300 ms Coordination Time Interval (CTI) between downstream line relays and upstream transformer backup relays, preventing false plant-wide blackouts.
  • Sensitive Ground Protection (ANSI 51N):
    Operating on a dedicated 20 / 1 A Neutral CT, the 51N relay easily senses the 7.27 A ground fault (0.364 A secondary) and trips in 1.75 s, stopping stator lamination burning.
Phase 6 Dynamic Modeling

6 · Station DC Battery Bank & Closed-Loop Breaker Trip Gating

  • Physical Battery Bank Model: Models 55 stationary lead-acid cells (110 V nominal, 200 Ah capacity, internal resistance 0.05 Ω) backed by a 20 kW charger floating at 123.75 V (2.25 V/cell).
  • Breaker Trip Gating Logic: The project enforces the vital real-world safety rule:
    Demand = TripRequest & dcHealthy
    If DC bus voltage drops below 88 V (0.80 pu), breaker trip coils cannot energize.
  • Trip Coil Energy Pulse: Sized at 2 kW for 0.2 s (400 Joules). When multiple breakers fire simultaneously during a master unit trip, DC bus voltage dips by only 1.0 V, proving the battery bank is sufficiently stiff to prevent DC brownout failure.
  • Emergency Blackout Autonomy: Under a complete station blackout (charger loss), the 200 Ah battery sustains continuous relay and communications loads for over 9.0 hours, fully meeting IEEE 485 standards.
Auxiliary System Study

7 · Radial vs. Looped Auxiliary Topology (GAT IN vs. GAT OUT)

  • Normal Radial Operation (GAT OUT):
    • GAT breaker is OPEN. The Unit Auxiliary Transformer (UAT) alone powers the 6.6 kV bus from the generator.
    • UAT carries 16.52 MVA (66.1% of its 25 MVA ONAF rating). Highly stable with zero circulating loops.
  • Transfer Looped Operation (GAT IN):
    • GAT breaker is CLOSED during startup or live bus transfer, creating a closed physical loop: 22 kV → GSUT → 230 kV GIS → GAT → 6.6 kV → UAT → 22 kV.
    • The Circulating Power Discovery: Due to differences in transformer impedances and tap changer positions, 5.9 MW of circulating power flows continuously around the loop.
    • UAT loading increases to 20.29 MVA (109.5% of its 19 MVA ONAN rating, requiring fans to run in ONAF mode).
  • Protection Interlock Requirement:
    During a 230 kV bus fault (F3) in the looped configuration, Relay 87B must trip BOTH Q0 and the GAT breaker (`P6_REQUESTS`). Otherwise, the fault would continue being fed backward from the generator through the auxiliary transformers!
Safety & Asset Protection

8 · Catastrophic Failures Prevented by the Defense System

  • Preventing Stator Core Iron Melting (F1): Without the 1,750.8 Ω NER, a 22 kV ground fault would draw over 100,000 A, melting stator copper and steel laminations into slag. The NER limits this to 7.27 A, meaning only cheap rewinding is needed instead of total machine scrapping.
  • Preventing Transformer Tank Rupture & Boiling Oil Fireball (F2): An electric arc at 126.21 kA vaporizes liquid mineral oil into hydrogen gas, building pressure at over 1,000 psi/s. Relay 87T trips in 45 ms, quenching the arc before the sealed steel tank can explode.
  • Preventing Switchyard Arc Plasma Explosion (F3): At 50.53 kA on the 230 kV bus, backup overcurrent takes 7.28 seconds. Fast busbar differential (87B) clears the fault in 35 ms, preventing the entire SF6 GIS hall from vaporizing.
  • Preventing Rotor Pole-Slipping & Shaft Fracture (F5): A sustained grid short circuit drops electrical export to 0 MW while the turbine pushes 450 MW. Fast line clearing (40 ms) and out-of-step protection (Relay 78) prevent violent torsional shockwaves from twisting and snapping the steel turbine shaft in two.
Scope Boundaries

9 · Main Project Limitations (To State Honestly in Viva)

  • Static Thevenin Grid (R = 0): PGCB utility grid is represented as a static 50 kA equivalent with infinite X/R. Conservative for plant voltage drops, but regional inter-area grid oscillations cannot be observed.
  • Lumped Static Auxiliary Load: The 14 MW auxiliary demand is lumped at 6.6 kV. High-inrush starting currents (5–6× FL) and stalling of large 400 V induction motors are not dynamically modelled.
  • GAT Delta Tertiary Omission: GAT 3.32 kV delta tertiary winding is open-circuited because manufacturer factory test sheets omitted pairwise impedances Z_PT and Z_ST.
  • Off-Nominal Turns Ratio: UAT and GAT secondary windings are rated 6.9 kV feeding a 6.6 kV bus (ratio a = 1.04545). Retained honestly as documented by Siemens rather than artificially forced to 1.0 pu.
  • Phasor-Domain Switchgear: Circuit breakers are modelled with 50 ms mechanical delay and breaking limit checks. Microsecond SF6 plasma arc restrikes, TRV, and RRRV require specialized EMT tools (PSCAD).
