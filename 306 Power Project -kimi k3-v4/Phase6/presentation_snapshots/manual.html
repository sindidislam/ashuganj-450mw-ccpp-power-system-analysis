02 / Electrical route

Follow power from the machine to the grid

In normal generation, mechanical input drives the generator at 22 kV. Power passes through the generator circuit breaker to the unit bus, then through the GSUT to 230 kV, Q0 and the GIS bus, the transmission line, and the PGCB receiving system. Auxiliary power branches from the unit bus through the UAT.

The GAT gives an alternative auxiliary supply from 230 kV. It is out of service in the normal GAT-out study baseline. The model lets the user change its service state; the drawing alone is not evidence of a complete automatic transfer sequence.

6.9 kV and 6.6 kV describe different things. UAT/GAT secondary winding nameplate ratings are 6.9 kV. The modeled auxiliary switchboard and load nominal basis are 6.6 kV. Keep both labels so a reader can distinguish equipment ratio from board operating designation.

The 450 MW plant title is separate from the generator’s 458 MVA rating and the default 360 MW study dispatch. MW measures real power; MVA measures apparent power. Neither number should silently replace the other.

04 / Subsystem guide

What each area contributes

These are the main views you open from the plant canvas. Nested measurement and busbar views are also included in the full reference.

Generator

Make electrical power at 22 kV

The synchronous machine converts mechanical input into three-phase electricity. Inner and terminal CT measurement boundaries define the generator differential zone. The generator breaker, 52G / GCB, separates the machine from the unit bus. A neutral grounding equivalent supplies an earth-fault return path.

Explore this subsystem ↗
Transformer

Raise 22 kV to 230 kV

GSUT 10BAT10 is the generator step-up transformer. Its 515 MVA, 230/22 kV YNd1 rating is source supported. LV and HV CTs define the transformer protection zone; the differential calculation accounts for turns ratio and phase shift.

Explore this subsystem ↗
Switchyard

Connect and protect the 230 kV junction

GSUT breaker Q0 connects the transformer to B_230, the GIS bus. The bus branches toward the line and GAT. Incoming, line and GAT CT boundaries support a current-balance check for bus differential protection.

Explore this subsystem ↗
Transmission Line

Carry power to the receiving station

Two parallel circuits connect the plant to the PGCB side. Each circuit is represented by a plant half and a remote half using PI sections. The selected line fault is placed at the midpoint of circuit 1. Local and remote aggregate CTs feed the added line differential study.

Explore this subsystem ↗
Grid

Represent the external power system

The PGCB voltage source and sequence impedance represent the external network at 230 kV. BGRID_230 marks the receiving bus. This is an equivalent boundary for the study, rather than a model of the complete national grid.

Explore this subsystem ↗
Auxiliaries

Keep plant services supplied

The UAT normally takes power from the unit-side 22 kV connection. The GAT provides the alternative supply from the 230 kV system. Both feed the common 6.6 kV switchboard equivalent, with 14 MW of aggregated plant demand. GAT is initially out in the normal baseline.

Explore this subsystem ↗
Measurements

Turn waveforms into readable quantities

Sensors provide actual network voltages and currents. Synchronized fundamental RMS and phasor processing supplies protection and displays. Nested groups organize generator/transformer, switchyard/line, auxiliary/neutral and fault channels; they do not add physical equipment.

Explore this subsystem ↗
Protection

Decide whether a zone needs isolation

Seven implemented elements compare measured currents with study thresholds and timing: GEN51, GSUT51, GEN51N, 87G, 87T, 87B and 87L. A relay trip is a request; the DC supply and breaker mechanism still determine whether the circuit opens.

Explore this subsystem ↗
DC Supply

Provide control and trip energy

A computed 110 V station DC equivalent combines battery, charger, loads and availability. Its output includes bus voltage, currents, state of charge and alarms. B06 identifies this computed bus within the behavior model; it is not a separate physical DC wiring network.

Explore this subsystem ↗
Breaker Control

Turn a request into switching action

Protection requests are combined with DC supply health and an assumed 50 ms mechanism. The outputs operate GCB, Q0, local line, remote line and GAT switches. Unit-trip output also tells the turbine and AVR controller to reduce their commands.

Explore this subsystem ↗
Turbine and AVR

Regulate speed and terminal voltage

A generic governor/turbine model changes mechanical power from rotor-speed feedback. The automatic voltage regulator changes field voltage from terminal-voltage feedback. These are dynamic study controllers driving the machine inputs, not recovered OEM control software.

Explore this subsystem ↗
Results

Collect evidence from the run

Plant quantities combines measured electrical, machine, protection, breaker and DC signals. Scopes display time histories; To Workspace blocks record original channels for export and analysis.

Explore this subsystem ↗

Recognize the important parts

Part or abbreviationMeaning in this modelRead it correctly
CT / VTCurrent transformer / voltage transformer: names for measurement boundaries supplying current and voltage data.The active blocks use ideal V/I measurements. CT ratio can define a relay’s current basis; magnetic core saturation, remanence, burden and VT error are not simulated.
GCB / 52G / Q0GCB and 52G identify the generator breaker. Q0 identifies the GSUT bay breaker at 230 kV. ANSI 52 means an AC circuit breaker.A Selector called Q0 is its signal channel; the Three-Phase Breaker is the actual circuit switch. A command is separate from observed current cessation.
GSUT / UAT / GATGenerator step-up transformer / unit auxiliary transformer / grid auxiliary transformer.GSUT exports power at 230 kV; UAT normally feeds auxiliaries from 22 kV; GAT supplies the alternative path from 230 kV.
YNd1 / Dyn11 / YNyn0d11Transformer vector-group notation. Y or y means star; D or d means delta; N or n denotes an accessible neutral. Clock numbers express relative phase displacement.Ratio and phase shift must be accounted for when currents on opposite transformer sides are compared. The source GAT includes a tertiary; the study uses a two-winding equivalent.
PI line sectionEquivalent series impedance and shunt capacitance of a line section.Two halves per circuit create a midpoint fault location. The active line representation does not reproduce every conductor or full mutual coupling.
Fault switchA deliberately inserted branch for a chosen short-circuit scenario.GEN, GSUT_LV, GSUT_HV, GIS230, LINE230, GRID230 and AUX66 are selectable locations; the fault branch is a study stimulus.

Auxiliary load groupOne of the aggregate loads consuming the modeled 14 MW total at 0.85 power factor.The motor-group names identify service areas. The blocks do not simulate each motor’s inertia, starting current or transient contribution. Omitted 400 V demand is included in the aggregate.
AVR / governorAutomatic voltage regulation changes excitation; the governor/turbine path changes mechanical power in response to speed.Generic study dynamics drive the real machine inputs. Source equipment existence does not establish these controller gains as OEM settings.
RMS / phasor / per unitRMS expresses an effective waveform magnitude; a complex phasor also carries phase angle; per unit divides a value by its stated base.Under balanced conditions, phase-to-neutral voltage is line voltage divided by √3: 22 kV corresponds to about 12.70 kV phase RMS. 06 / Protection and trip sequence

A relay request still needs trip energy

Network fault → CT/VT measurement → pickup and relay timing → trip request → usable DC and mechanism → breaker command → changed physical circuit. These are distinct steps. A latched relay request does not prove that a breaker opened or that fault current stopped.

CodePlain meaningDynamic model and source distinction
51Time-delayed phase overcurrent: sustained current above pickup causes a trip, with faster response at higher current for an inverse curve.GEN51 and GSUT51 are implemented. Source generator / transformer protection drawings identify overcurrent functions; this project supplies the executable algorithm, pickup values and delays.
51NNeutral or earth overcurrent: detects current in the neutral/earth return path.GEN51N uses the measured physical generator neutral current. The generator drawing explicitly lists 51N; the particular equivalent grounding, threshold and timer are study implementation choices.
87GGenerator differential: compare current at the two boundaries of the generator zone; an internal fault creates an imbalance.Implemented using synchronized phasor differences, a pickup floor, bias and delay. 87G is explicit in the source drawing; the model does not reproduce a winding-resolved stator or OEM relay firmware.
87TTransformer differential: compare the compensated currents entering/leaving the transformer zone.Implemented with ratio/vector compensation and biased differential thresholds. The source labels transformer differential as generic 87; 87T is the project’s descriptive function name. Live harmonic blocking and high-set stages are absent.
87BBus differential: the signed sum of currents at the bus boundaries should balance during healthy through-current.Implemented using incoming minus line minus GAT current at the GIS zone. The source GIS interface explicitly shows 87B; project settings and execution are added.
87LLine differential: compare current at the local and remote ends of the protected line.ADDED BY US Implemented as a study function. No explicit 87L was identified in the 13 supplied PDFs; a referenced detailed South GIS drawing was absent. There is no claim that this is the installed line scheme.
50BFBreaker-failure protection: when the intended breaker fails to clear, a backup scheme trips other breakers.The source shows a GIS 87B–50BF interface. 50BF backup logic is not implemented. A failed-breaker scenario can prevent opening, but does not provide detection and backup isolation.

Which trip requests go where

ElementsRequested action
GEN51, GSUT51, GEN51N, 87G, 87TUnit trip, GCB open and Q0 open.
87BQ0, local line breaker and GAT open.
87LLocal and remote line breakers open.

The mechanism delay is an assumed 50 ms. Relay delay, mechanism time and observed current cessation are different quantities. The added 87L has a 5 ms aggregate communication/processing allowance; it does not simulate a communications channel, loss of samples or channel failure.

Only the seven stated elements are active dynamic protection. Other source-listed functions—such as distance, reverse power, rotor earth, 64G, intertripping and complete plant backup logic—are not automatically implemented because a source document or equipment list mentions them. The separate analytical relay plots also do not add high-set or harmonic logic to this model.

For exact saved numerical defaults, controls and exported event definitions, use the operating manual. This presentation guide focuses on meaning and provenance; current run metadata identifies the settings actually used.

07 / Station DC and model scope

What the DC and auxiliary equivalents include

The supplied plant SLD contains several levels, including 110 V, 220 V and 24 V DC systems and 400 V low-voltage distribution. This model aggregates only a 110 V station DC control/trip equivalent; the other source systems are not independently connected or solved.

B06 / computed supply

Battery and charger

The 110 V, 55-cell, 200 Ah study bank supplies a computed DC balance with battery resistance, state of charge, charger limits and source availability. The charger also requires adequate measured auxiliary AC voltage.

Bank capacity, cell count, charger dynamics, thresholds and load behavior are project assumptions, not recovered installed-bank nameplate data.

B6.6 / electrical AC board

Plant auxiliaries

The UAT and GAT feed actual electrical load equivalents at the common auxiliary bus. The 14 MW total includes the modeled service groups and the allowance for omitted low-voltage demand.

No separate 400 V terminals or individual motor dynamics can be inferred from these aggregate loads.

DC measurements include voltage, source and load currents, battery state of charge, and availability alarms. The initial aggregate trip demand draws modeled coil power. Individual coils are not independently wired or measured, and a later request while aggregate demand remains asserted does not create a new separate coil pulse.

Loss of DC can leave a valid relay request unable to open a breaker. Restoration can let a pending initial trip proceed. This behavior explains why the DC chain belongs in the protection story, even though B06 is represented by equations rather than a physical DC circuit.

Presentation scope: the snapshot set covers all exported model records, not every component drawn in the real plant SLD. Equipment aggregation, omitted feeders and numerical additions are stated explicitly so a reader can describe the model without claiming a complete digital replica of the plant.

Added by this project

The Simulink subsystem structure, numerical circuit equivalents, fault switches, ideal measurement processing, seven relay implementations and study settings, DC balance model, generic controls, assumed breaker mechanisms, result logging and presentation busbar labels/colors were added for this study. They use selected documented equipment facts where available.

Other deliberate simplifications include the generator neutral/zero-sequence equivalent; transformer terminal-zone fault surrogates; two-winding GAT equivalent; aggregate auxiliary loads; finite external-grid equivalent; and omission of detailed breaker arc/TRV, CT saturation, complete communications, detailed motor/LV networks and the full installed protection inventory.

These source pages establish equipment and function context. Commissioned relay settings, installed breaker performance and unmodeled protection functions cannot be inferred from the executable study values.
